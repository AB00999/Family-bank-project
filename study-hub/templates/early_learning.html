A caregiver-led lesson · birth to 3 years

Colors around us

Share a short story, notice colors together, and give little learners time to look, gesture, move, or use words.

10–15 flexible minutes 1:1 with a trusted adult No worksheets or special purchases

What we're practicing

Little steps that support learning

  • Listen to and hear a few simple color words.
  • Notice, look toward, reach for, or choose between familiar objects.
  • Enjoy a shared back-and-forth with a caring adult.

Every child learns at their own pace. Looking, smiling, moving, making sounds, communicating with gestures or a device, and quietly watching are all ways to join in.

Gather together

Simple materials

  • One sturdy board book or a large picture with a few clear colors.
  • Two or three large, intact, age-appropriate soft toys or blocks in different colors.
  • A clear spot on the floor or at a stable table, with a trusted adult close by.

Use familiar items you already have. Check toy age labels and inspect each item before play.

Safety first

Stay close and keep it simple

Keep the child within arm's reach the whole time. Use only large items with no loose or detachable parts. Keep small objects, magnets, button batteries, balloons, and plastic bags away. Pause if the child is tired or uncomfortable; never leave a child alone with lesson materials.

The lesson

Look, find, and tell

  1. 1–2 min

    Say hello

    Get comfortable together. Hold up one safe object and say, “Hello, blue ball!” Use a warm voice and give the child time to notice it.

  2. 2–3 min

    Read and notice

    Look at a board book or picture. Point to one color and name it. Pause after each page so the child can look, gesture, vocalize, or turn away for a break.

  3. 3–4 min

    Find a color

    Offer two large safe objects, one at a time. Say, “Here is red. Can you find red?” Model pointing or reaching; there is no need to test or correct the child.

  4. 2–3 min

    Take turns

    Let the child choose an object by looking, reaching, pointing, or using their usual communication. Copy a safe movement or sound, then wait for their turn.

  5. 1–2 min

    Review and finish

    Repeat the color words you shared. Say, “We looked at red and blue together!” Celebrate curiosity, then put materials away together.

Make it fit the child

Age and communication adaptations

Birth–11 months

Keep it brief and follow the baby's cues. The adult holds the book or object, names what the baby is looking at, and stops when the baby needs a break.

12–23 months

Offer one object at a time and name its color. Give plenty of wait time; looking, touching, sounds, and gestures all count.

24–47 months

Offer a choice between two objects. Invite the child to find, match, or name a color, and let them lead the conversation or repeat the game.

Use the child's home language and familiar words. Adapt for vision, hearing, movement, sensory, or communication needs, and follow any guidance from the child's family or educator.

For families and educators

Notice what interests the child

After play, remember one thing the child enjoyed and one word, gesture, sound, or choice they shared. Use that interest to plan another short story or play activity. This is a playful learning activity, not a test or developmental assessment.
